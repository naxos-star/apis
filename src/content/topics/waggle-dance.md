---
title: The waggle dance
summary: Foragers tell nestmates where to find flowers using a figure-eight dance on the comb.
order: 2
---
When a forager finds a rich patch of flowers, she returns to the dark hive and dances on the vertical comb. Karl von Frisch decoded this behaviour, work that earned him a Nobel Prize in 1973.

The dance has two parts: a straight **waggle run** where the bee shakes her abdomen, and a return loop that is repeated on alternating sides.

- **Direction:** the angle of the waggle run relative to vertical matches the angle between the sun and the food source.
- **Distance:** a longer waggle run means a farther source.
- **Quality:** a more vigorous, repeated dance advertises a better source.

Nestmates follow the dancer in the dark, pick up the information through touch and scent, and fly out to find the flowers themselves.
