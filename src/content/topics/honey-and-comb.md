---
title: Honey and wax comb
summary: Nectar becomes honey through enzymes and evaporation, then is stored in hexagonal wax cells.
order: 3
---
Foragers carry nectar in a special honey stomach. Back at the hive, house bees pass it mouth to mouth, adding enzymes that break down its sugars. They then spread it in thin layers and fan it with their wings until most of the water has evaporated. Once ripe, the honey is sealed under a wax cap.

The container is **comb**: rows of hexagonal cells that bees build from wax secreted by glands on their abdomens. Hexagons tile with no gaps and use little wax for the volume they hold. The same cells store honey and pollen, and the colony also raises brood in them.

Honey is the colony's winter food supply. Beekeepers harvest only the surplus, leaving enough for the bees to survive the cold months.
