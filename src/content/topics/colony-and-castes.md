---
title: The colony and its castes
summary: One queen, thousands of workers and a few hundred drones make up a single superorganism.
order: 1
---
A honey bee colony is built from three kinds of bees.

**The queen** is the only fully fertile female. A productive queen can lay well over a thousand eggs a day in peak season, and she steers the colony with a blend of pheromones.

**Workers** are sterile females and make up nearly the whole population, from a few thousand in winter to tens of thousands in summer. Over a lifetime a worker moves through jobs: cleaning cells, nursing larvae, building wax, guarding the entrance and finally foraging. Summer workers live about six weeks; winter bees can survive for months.

**Drones** are males. They don't forage or sting. Their one job is to mate with queens from other colonies, and they are evicted from the hive when autumn food runs short.

When a colony outgrows its home, the old queen leaves with about half the workers in a **swarm**, while a new queen emerges to take over the hive.
